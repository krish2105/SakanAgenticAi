## RDC-1: Jurisdiction

The Rental Dispute Center handles disputes arising from registered tenancy contracts — rent increases, eviction notices, maintenance obligations, and security deposit returns — for both residential and commercial leases. It does not have jurisdiction over sale-and-purchase disputes between a buyer and developer, which instead route through the off-plan complaint mechanism referencing the relevant Form F agreement, or through standard civil courts for ready-property sale disputes.

## RDC-2: Mandatory Tenancy Contract Registration

A tenancy contract is only enforceable before the Center if it has been registered in the official tenancy contract registry at or shortly after signing. An unregistered lease materially weakens a landlord's eviction claim and a tenant's claim for security deposit or maintenance disputes alike, since the Center will generally decline to hear a dispute over an unregistered tenancy's terms without independent corroborating evidence.

## RDC-3: Rent Increase Caps

Rent increases upon renewal are capped according to a published index comparing the current rent to the average market rent for comparable units in the same building or community; increases beyond the capped percentage are not enforceable even where the tenant nominally agreed to them in a renewal notice, unless the tenant independently petitions to waive the cap protection, which the Center scrutinizes closely.

## RDC-4: Eviction for Owner Use or Sale

A landlord may terminate a tenancy for personal use, sale of the property, or major renovation only by providing a minimum 12-month advance notice via registered mail or notary, explicitly citing one of the recognized grounds. Eviction notices citing "sale of the property" while a Form F or Oqood filing shows the unit was not actually transferred within a reasonable period following eviction may be challenged by the former tenant as pretextual.

## RDC-5: Security Deposit Disputes

Security deposits must be returned within the timeframe specified in the tenancy contract (commonly 14–30 days after vacancy and a joint condition inspection), net of documented damage beyond normal wear and tear. Disputed deductions are resolved by the Center based on a comparative move-in/move-out condition report; a landlord unable to produce a move-in report bears the burden of proving claimed damage was not pre-existing.
