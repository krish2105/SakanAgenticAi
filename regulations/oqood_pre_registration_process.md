## OQOOD-1: Purpose of Oqood Pre-Registration

Oqood is the interim registration of a buyer's off-plan unit interest in the regulator's system, issued once a Form F sale agreement is lodged and the corresponding initial payment is confirmed in escrow. It is not a title deed — it records a contractual interest pending project completion — but it is the mechanism through which a buyer's claim to the unit becomes visible to third parties, including subsequent mortgage lenders and any competing purchaser.

## OQOOD-2: Required Filing Steps

Pre-registration requires: the registered Form F sale agreement number; proof of the initial escrow payment; the buyer's passport or Emirates ID; and, where the buyer is financing the purchase, a no-objection letter from the mortgage lender confirming the lender's interest will be separately noted. The developer, not the buyer, is responsible for submitting the Oqood filing within 60 days of Form F registration; a developer that fails to file within this window is in breach independent of any construction-related obligation.

## OQOOD-3: Effect of Oqood on Resale and Financing

Only a unit with an active Oqood registration may be legally resold (assigned) or used as security for an off-plan mortgage. A buyer attempting to assign or mortgage an unregistered interest has no protected claim recognized by the regulator, and any such transaction is unenforceable against the escrow account or the eventual title deed.

## OQOOD-4: Conversion to Title Deed

Upon project completion, unit handover, and the developer's submission of a completion certificate, the Oqood pre-registration converts to a full title deed under the Title Deed Transfer Process. The buyer is not required to re-file underlying documents already verified at Oqood stage, though any change of buyer identity since pre-registration (e.g., inheritance, corporate restructuring) must be separately documented before conversion.
