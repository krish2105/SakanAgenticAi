## RERA-A-1: Purpose and Scope of Form A

Form A is the initial project registration filing a developer must lodge with the regulator before marketing, reserving, or selling any unit in a proposed development. It establishes the legal existence of the project in the regulator's system, links the project to a registered land parcel, and is the precondition for opening an escrow account under the Escrow Account Regulations. A project without an approved Form A on file may not appear in any official project registry, and any unit reservation taken against it is void from inception. Developers must file a separate Form A for each distinct project, even where multiple projects share a single master-development name or a single developer entity.

## RERA-A-2: Required Supporting Documents

An admissible Form A submission must include: (a) proof of land ownership or a valid long-term lease instrument covering the project footprint; (b) an approved construction permit from the relevant municipal planning authority; (c) a feasibility study demonstrating the developer's financial capacity to complete the project; (d) the developer's trade license and, where the developer is a special-purpose entity, its parent guarantee; and (e) an undertaking to open a dedicated escrow account with a regulator-approved trustee bank prior to any sales activity. Incomplete filings are returned without a registration number and do not toll any developer obligation.

## RERA-A-3: Registration Number and Public Listing

Upon approval, the regulator issues a unique project registration number in the format RERA-OP-{year}-{sequence}, which must appear on all subsequent Form B reservations, Form F sale agreements, and marketing collateral for the project. The project is simultaneously added to the public off-plan project registry, listing the developer, escrow trustee bank, and registration status. Any change to escrow trustee, project scope, or unit count exceeding 10% of the original filing requires a Form A amendment before further sales may proceed.

## RERA-A-4: Grounds for Suspension

The regulator may suspend a project's Form A registration where: escrow account contributions fall below the percentage of construction completion reported by the appointed engineering consultant; the developer fails two consecutive quarterly progress inspections; or a material misstatement is found in the original feasibility study. Suspension freezes new unit reservations and sale agreement registrations for the project until the underlying deficiency is remediated and re-verified.
