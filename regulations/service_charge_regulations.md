## SERVICE-1: Basis for Service Charge Calculation

Annual service charges must be calculated from an itemized budget submitted by the building's owners' association or managing developer to the regulator for approval before the charge year begins, broken down by category (maintenance, utilities for common areas, insurance, reserve fund contribution, management fees). A charge rate not derived from an approved budget is not enforceable against unit owners, and any dispute over an unapproved rate should be flagged rather than treated as a routine payable.

## SERVICE-2: Reserve Fund Contribution

A minimum percentage of total service charges, set relative to the building's age and the findings of its most recent reserve fund study, must be allocated to a long-term reserve fund for major capital replacements (roofing, façade, mechanical systems). Buildings without a current reserve fund study on file are presumed under-reserved for compliance-summary purposes, which is a relevant risk flag for any valuation relying on the building's stated service charge rate remaining stable.

## SERVICE-3: Owner Voting on Budget Variance

Any service charge increase exceeding the prior year's approved budget by more than a specified threshold requires an owners' association vote before the increase may be levied, unless the increase is solely attributable to a regulator-mandated insurance or utility cost pass-through, which is exempt from the vote requirement but must still be separately itemized and disclosed.

## SERVICE-4: Non-Payment and Liens

Unpaid service charges accrue as a lien against the unit and must be cleared, along with any late-payment penalty, before a No-Objection Certificate can be issued for a sale transfer under the Title Deed Transfer Process. A buyer's compliance check should always confirm service charge arrears status for the subject unit, since an outstanding lien blocks transfer regardless of the sale agreement's own terms.

## SERVICE-5: Per-Sqft Disclosure in Marketing

Where a unit's service charge rate is disclosed in marketing or a sale listing, it must be presented as an AED-per-square-foot annual rate matching the most recently approved budget, not a stale or estimated figure from a prior charge year, to avoid materially misleading a prospective buyer on ongoing holding costs.
